Derived from `Claude_Skill_-_Performance_Copywriting/validated-structures.md`
(Dataset 1, 361 ads, ~$89.5k est. spend, DE market, Sept 2026).

**Placeholders:** `{{BRAND}}`, `{{DOSE}}`, `{{PRICE}}`, `{{GUARANTEE_DAYS}}`,

## 1. What to launch, and why exactly this

The source data says the winning portfolio is **narrow**: $14.1k came from
4 ads, while 36 ads of the filler angle made $5.8k. So launch **4 angles ×
1 creative**, not 40 clones. Add clones only after an angle proves out.

| # | Angle | Modeled on | Evidence | Traffic temp | → Landing page |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Universal provocation | `1979449889375268` | $339/day, $14.1k from 4 ads — best per-ad efficiency in the set | Cold / unaware | **Listicle** |
| **B** | Third-person POV story | `1374898884815000` | $260/day, $9.5k | Cold / problem-aware | **Offer page** |
| **C** | Symptom alert (control) | `1684769269495401` | $167/day sustained **49 days**, 53 ads, 4 pages | Broad / problem-aware | **Offer page** |
| **D** | Short benefit stack (video) | `27958249043840794` | $245/day at day 10, cheapest to produce | Warm / retarget | **PDP** |

**Angle-to-LP pairing is not interchangeable.** In the dataset, the
provocation hook sends $9.1k to the listicle and never to the v3 offer page,
while the symptom-alert hook sends $12.7k to the v3 offer page and only $1.7k
to the listicle. Cold provocation needs an explanation page before it can be
asked to buy. Respect the pairing.

**Test order** (per `SKILL.md` hierarchy): hook first. A/B/C/D are four
different *angles*, so each needs 2-3 hook variants of its own — see §3.

**Do not** open with the filler angle ("Eliminiere Parasiten & Pilze"). It
looks dominant on ad count and peaks at $31/day. It is a late-stage cheap
evergreen, not a launch angle.

```
👉 {{LP_URL}}
```

> **Headline** (40 max): `Reizdarm? Vielleicht nicht.` (27)
> **Description** (30 max): `5 Dinge, die kaum jemand sagt` (29)

## 4. Landing pages

The dataset's clearest finding: **budget migrated across three versions of the
same offer page** ($2.1k on v1 → $18.9k on v2 → $25.9k on v3), while a
listicle ran in parallel at $15.8k. They won by rewriting the page, not by
adding creatives. Build for that from day one.

**Launch with two pages, not one:**

1. **Listicle** — `5-gruende-…` equivalent, for Angle A cold traffic.
   Structure: 5 numbered reasons, reason 3 carries the blind-test argument
   (beat 7), reason 4 the dosage argument (beat 9), offer block at the end
   plus one mid-scroll.
2. **Offer page** — for Angles B and C. Full 12-beat run from
   `validated-structures.md`, offer block above the fold *and* after the
   mechanism section.

Plus your existing **PDP** for Angle D.

**Then iterate the offer page on a version ladder** (`-v1`, `-v2`, `-v3` in
the slug, exactly as they did) so ad-level data stays attributable to a page
version. Treat the page as a test axis equal to the hook — in this dataset it
moved more money than any single creative.

## 5. Claims you cannot carry over

The source ads are not a compliance model. Specifically, do not port:

- **"Vernichtet Bakterien, Pilze & Parasiten"** / "Eliminiere Parasiten" —
  an antiparasitic effect claim on a food supplement. There is no authorised
  EU health claim for oregano or black seed oil; Reg. (EC) 1924/2006 permits
  only claims on the Union register.
- **"Klinisch geprüfte Inhaltsstoffe, die Toxine ausleiten & Entzündungen
  reduzieren"**, **"in nur 7 Tagen"** — unauthorised claims plus an
  unsupported timeframe.
- **"20.000+ zufriedene Kunden"**, **"137.000 zufriedene Kunden"**,
  **"4,8/5 auf Trustpilot"** — reproduced verbatim across 53 ads. Use your
  real figures or none; a fabricated review score is a straightforward
  UCPD violation and Trustpilot enforces on it separately.
- **The persona network** — pages posing as pharmacy journals, health
  gazettes and named doctors. This is the single highest-risk element in the
  source account: it puts the domain and the business manager at stake, not
  just individual ads.
- **Inconsistent guarantees** — 30 days in most creatives, 90 in others.
  Pick one and hold it everywhere, or eat the chargebacks.

**The honest trade-off:** a version of this that stays inside EU supplement
claim rules cannot say the product kills parasites. What it *can* do is run
the entire persuasive engine — beats 5, 7 and 9 — because none of those three
are product claims. They are claims about the *diagnostic process*: that a
junk label was applied, that a single negative test is not an all-clear, and
that unstandardised retail oils vary wildly in active content. All three are
defensible, and per the dataset they are what carries the argument. The
product claim is the least load-bearing part of the whole structure.

## 6. First-week read

| Metric | Kill below | Scale above |
|---|---|---|
| Hook rate (3-sec / impr.) | 20 % | 30 % |
| CTR (link) | 0.9 % | 1.8 % |
| LP → ATC | — | check page version, not creative |

Sequence:
1. **Day 1-3** — 4 angles live, even budget, one creative each. Judge on hook
   rate and CTR only; sales volume is too thin to read.
2. **Day 3** — kill any angle under 20 % hook rate. Do not iterate a dead
   hook; replace the angle.
3. **Day 4-7** — for each surviving angle, add its 2 remaining hook variants.
   Now you are testing hooks inside a proven angle, which is where the 60-70 %
   leverage sits.
4. **Week 2** — only now clone the winner across creatives, and start the
   offer-page v2. Judge angle-vs-page separately; the source data shows the
   page was worth more than the creative.
